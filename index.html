MONEY ACADEMY

How money actually works

Not "get rich" content. The mechanics — so a wage, a client payment, or a bad purchase all get evaluated the same way.

1. The four jobs of every euro

  1. Survive — essentials: food, transport, phone, gear.
  2. Protect — a buffer so one bad week doesn't wreck you.
  3. Grow — skills and investments that compound.
  4. Enjoy — spending on things you actually value, on purpose.

2. Income ≠ wealth

Someone earning big and spending big can end up with nothing. Someone earning modestly and directing it well ends up ahead. Track:

income → fixed costs → variable costs → savings → investing → discretionary

3. Compounding is patience, not prediction

Wealth is contributions + time + return, repeated without interruption. The lesson isn't "pick the right stock" — it's "don't stop showing up and don't withdraw early."

4. Status objects vs. assets

A watch or a car can be a genuine reward. It's not automatically an asset. Ask: does this produce value, preserve value, or improve my ability to earn — or does it just burn cash?

Decision filters — run before you act

Before you spend

Ask in order: need, useful, joy, status, impulse. If the honest answer is status or impulse — wait 24 hours before buying.

Before you invest

What's the actual asset? What makes it fall? What fees or tax apply? What's your time horizon? Could you explain it to a mate in one paragraph?

A high income with no saving rate is a treadmill. A modest income with a real saving rate and time behind it is a flywheel.

PEOPLE ACADEMY

Reading and dealing with people

Understanding what drives someone is a communication skill, not a manipulation tactic. Use it to be clearer, not sneakier.

Listen like you're gathering intel

Every conversation carries four layers: facts, emotion, motive, and what's not being said. Ask before you assume — "what matters most to you here?" beats guessing.

What actually drives people

moneystatusbelonging securityautonomyrecognition conveniencefear of loss

Conversation formula

ask → listen → reflect → clarify → respond

Don't build your reply while the other person is still talking. That's the tell that you weren't actually listening.

Conflict formula

observation → impact → need → request

Separate the person from the problem. One disagreement is not a verdict on someone's whole character.

Sales, negotiation, and difficult conversations are the same skill wearing different uniforms: find the real problem, communicate the value straight, remove uncertainty, let them decide.

Client discovery questions

  • What are you doing now?
  • What isn't working?
  • What does that actually cost you?
  • What would success look like?
  • Why deal with it now?
  • What have you already tried?

REFERENCE

Field manual

The principles, condensed. Read this when you forget why you're doing any of this.

Money principles

  • Income is not wealth.
  • High income with high consumption still builds nothing.
  • Liquidity matters — life is uncertain.
  • Diversification reduces dependence on one outcome.
  • Fees, taxes, and risk are real costs, not fine print.
  • Investing and speculation are not the same activity.

People principles

  • Better questions beat better answers.
  • People remember how a conversation felt.
  • Confidence is clarity, not volume.
  • Boundaries are a form of communication.
  • Negotiation starts with understanding the other side's incentives.
  • Trust is built by consistency, not by a single big gesture.

Business principles

  • Sell the outcome, not the task.
  • A lead system beats a one-off client.
  • Proof closes deals better than persuasion does.
  • Activity is not the same thing as progress.
  • Package your skill stack — don't sell it piece by piece.

12-month target

Become the version of yourself who can: earn from a real skill, manage cash without panic, explain investing simply, sell without pressure, negotiate calmly, handle a hard conversation, run a repeatable system, and keep the promises you make to yourself.

Skills → proof → experience → cash flow → assets → leverage → long-term wealth. Ambition is not the same as guaranteed success — but it's the only starting point that works.
